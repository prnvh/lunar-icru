# Initial literature and evidence review

**Review date:** 2026-09-29

**Scope:** historical lunar-propellant economic studies, model comparability, reconstruction evidence, and present-day evidence that constrains a future scenario space.

This is an evidence map for the project, not a completed systematic review and not a record of completed model reconstructions. The cited source links were checked during this review. Candidate inclusion remains provisional until source packets, equations, and result reproductions are inspected.

## Main findings

1. The disagreement in the literature is not one result expressed in different units. The studies ask different questions: commercial profitability, government campaign cost, the price needed to attract a customer, or the physical/economic opportunity cost of production. They also place propellant at different destinations and include different systems in their boundaries.
2. A faithful comparison therefore needs two layers: preserve each study's native decision metric and system boundary, then compare only the subset of outputs that can be mapped to a precisely specified shared decision question. A blanket conversion of every result into a single `$ / kg` figure would erase some of the disagreement the paper is meant to explain.
3. The source set in `paper.md` is promising but is currently author-oriented. Authors sometimes have multiple papers or variants, and some later studies explicitly rebuild or critique earlier models. The unit of selection should be a versioned model and decision case, not an author name.
4. Primary-source availability is unusually good for several candidates: full NASA PDFs for Jones, the USRA/NASA archive for Kornuta, the SEI PDF for Charania and DePasquale, and full papers or reports for Sowers and Blair. Availability of the original executable spreadsheets or software is a separate question and must not be inferred from availability of the paper.
5. Current technology evidence supports scenario analysis, not a single forecast. NASA has demonstrated oxygen extraction from lunar-soil simulant in terrestrial vacuum testing, while mine-scale ice grade/distribution and integrated, long-duration production remain uncertain. GAO reported in July 2026 that orbital propellant storage and transfer for SpaceX's lunar landing plan had not yet been demonstrated as of May 2026.

## What is in the repository

`paper.md` sets a fixed question and a staged experiment: native-study reproduction, common exogenous inputs, physical-architecture harmonization, model-form substitutions, and a modern range-based stress test. `rules.md` adds sound safeguards: preserve original logic, parameter provenance, native outputs, explicit ambiguities, predefine benchmark cases, and avoid tuning for convergence or disagreement. As of the reconstruction checkpoint, `models/jones2020`, `models/kornuta2019`, and `models/sowers_niac2020` contain independent implementations and source-derived parameter records. Jones's campaign ratio and Sowers's original IRR remain unresolved; Kornuta's constant-cash-flow NPVs are a close match to the displayed table. There is still no parameter crosswalk, common benchmark, or harmonized result. The map below remains research planning and evidence collection, not a claim that those three cases are fully reproduced or that the inclusion set is frozen.

### Related literature that should not be mistaken for independent model implementations

- **Sowers & Dreyer (2019), _Ice Mining in Lunar Permanently Shadowed Regions_** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1089/space.2019.0002)) describes thermal-mining architecture and is useful for technical assumptions. Sowers's 2021 business case is the more explicit financial case.
- **Bennett's three papers above** reuse or extend Jones, Kornuta, and Charania inputs. Their structural changes are scientifically useful, but they must be represented as versioned descendants with shared-source provenance, not counted as three independent baseline models.
- **Coutts & Sowers (2025), _Value of Information for Lunar Ice Exploration_** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1089/space.2024.0045)) connects deposit uncertainty to cash-flow value and can inform future uncertainty framing; it is not a direct propellant-model replication.
- **Shishko, Stoica & Mrozinski (2019), _Business Case Analysis of Lunar Thermal Mining_** ([NASA cost symposium presentation](https://www.iceaaonline.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Shishko-Lunar-Thermal-Mining.pdf)) offers a separate financial-analysis framework with reliability, maintainability, replacement, depreciation, and yearly cash-flow concerns. Use it as a completeness cross-check, not as if it were Sowers's model.
- **Sommariva et al. (2020), _The economics of moon mining_** ([article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2020.03.066)) and later lifecycle-cost work may widen the candidate pool. They need a source audit before inclusion because they may evaluate different products or system boundaries.

## What the literature establishes—and does not

The studies' headline conclusions span both sides of commercial competitiveness. Jones's cislunar Mars case reports a higher lunar-propellant cost under its assumptions. Bennett's follow-on shows that system aggregation and nonlinear scale assumptions can reverse a comparison. Kornuta, Sowers and Charania frame company economics and customer cases; Pelech uses a different opportunity-cost metric. Metzger's 2023 paper argues the disagreement was partly caused by choices of capital-transport architecture and insufficient lifetime output. Those are not yet controlled results: they differ in customers, demand, destinations, technologies, costs, study years, financing and model structure.

This makes the proposed staged experiment appropriate, with two amendments:

1. **Predeclare an estimand per experiment.** Define a common customer, delivered product/state/location, period, counterfactual Earth supply chain, and included cost boundary before running adapters. “Same launch cost” alone is not enough if one model delivers to GEO and another to a lunar depot.
2. **Make metric compatibility a gate.** Retain each study's native metric in all reports. Where models can answer the exact same benchmark question, add a transparent adapter or common decision indicator. If no valid mapping exists, mark the model not comparable for that experiment; do not manufacture a common dollar-per-kilogram figure.

For causal attribution, the current input → architecture → model-form decomposition is a good organizing scheme, but results should be described as staged, conditional changes in the selected disagreement statistic. Add one-factor structural substitutions only where a model permits them. Since inputs and architecture interact (for example, demand changes utilization and scale), any additive percentages must be treated as interaction-sensitive; a small factorial or permutation/Shapley analysis is a stronger option if the runs and evidence support it. The GAO cost-estimating guidance supports explicit technical baselines, ground rules, traceable data, sensitivity/risk analysis, validation, and enough documentation for independent recreation ([GAO-20-195G](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-20-195g)). It is guidance for estimate credibility, not a claim that space-project forecasts can be made accurate by documentation alone.

NASA's [Model and Simulation Standard NASA-STD-7009B (5 March 2024)](https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/B/1/NASA-STD-7009B-Final-3-5-2024.pdf) distinguishes implementation verification, conceptual validation, empirical validation, input pedigree and uncertainty. In this project, matching a paper's outputs establishes reconstruction fidelity; it does not empirically validate a future lunar mine or market. Keep those claims separate. A possible shared quantity is one usable kilogram of a named product at a named destination, date and thermodynamic state, but a mission-service comparison may be required when products or customer services are not equivalent per kilogram.

For a simplified pre-tax, constant-real-price case, a harmonized levelized delivered price could be calculated as `p* = sum(C_t/(1+r)^t) / sum(q_t,usable,delivered/(1+r)^t)`. It must not replace a source's native IRR, NPV, cumulative program cost, required sale price, or opportunity-cost metric. When financing, taxes, subsidies, depreciation, residual value or nonlinear revenue matter, calculate from the source-consistent cash flow instead. Record price year, real/nominal convention and discount rate explicitly.

## Modern evidence boundary (as of 2026-09-29)

- **Resource geology:** orbital and remote sensing establish evidence for lunar polar water/hydrogen, but they do not yet give a mine-ready grade, depth, physical form, continuity, and recoverability at the scale needed to design and cost a plant. NASA describes VIPER's planned role as characterizing distribution and physical state ([NASA VIPER science goals](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/viper/science-and-exploration/)); USGS's 2022 assessment explains why orbital neutron data cannot locate deposits precisely ([USGS Circular 1507](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/1507/cir1507.pdf)). Thus resource concentration and extraction yield belong in explicit ranges or alternative deposit cases, not a single “known lunar ice” input.
- **ISRU process demonstrations:** NASA reported in 2025 that a vacuum-chamber campaign melted about 25 kg of lunar-soil simulant and measured molecular oxygen from molten regolith electrolysis ([NASA Kennedy](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/kennedy/nasa-kennedy-breathes-life-into-moon-soil-testing/)). This is a meaningful technology milestone, but it is not an integrated lunar water-to-cryogenic-H2/LOX plant, commercial uptime, mine-grade test, or cost estimate. NASA's 2023 lunar-water assessment likewise lays out the extraction, capture, purification and electrolysis chain and notes the need to compare process steps at system level ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230005189)).
- **Carbothermal subsystem evidence:** [Haggerty et al. (ICES 2025), _Environmental Testing of a Fully Automated Carbothermal Reactor for Lunar Oxygen Production_](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250004151) reports thermal-vacuum testing of flight-like hardware and oxygen extraction from lunar regolith simulant. This is subsystem demonstration evidence; it does not include the complete plant, downstream oxygen finishing, lunar feedstock variability, long-term availability, or delivered-product economics. Do not transfer reactor performance directly into a full plant's $/kg.
- **Reusable space logistics:** as of GAO's July 23, 2026 report, SpaceX had not demonstrated orbital propellant storage/transfer required for its lunar landing plan, as of May 2026 ([GAO-26-108556](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108556/index.html)). Use this only as evidence of a present program risk; it does not imply that every future transfer system will fail. Do not use projected vendor prices or performance as observed costs.
- **Implication for benchmark design:** define a parameter space with source-backed bounds where evidence permits and scenario labels where it does not. Keep demonstrator measurements, engineering extrapolations, program targets, and analyst-selected stress cases in separate provenance classes. Do not use one vehicle-specific forecast as the study's economic baseline.

For production-side scenario anchors, [Kleinhenz & Paz (2021), _A Lunar Water ISRU System Study for Human-Scale Propellant Production_](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210016820) documents an engineering baseline of 5 wt% water, 75% extraction efficiency, 20 cm overburden and a 4.9 t/68 kW equipment estimate for a specified oxygen/hydrogen output. The source excludes important support systems (including power generation/transmission and thermal management); use those values as traceable design assumptions, not observed plant performance. [NASA's 2024 fission surface power concept](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/glenn/nasas-fission-surface-power-project-energizes-lunar-exploration/) states design goals of 40 kWe, under 6 t and ten years; these are goals, not a demonstrated lunar generator or a guarantee of ISRU system life.

## Recommended inclusion workflow

Apply the repository's formal rule to each **versioned model case**, and score four separate dimensions before deciding inclusion:

1. **Primary-result recoverability:** Is the principal result unambiguous, with enough equations/tables to reproduce it?
2. **Calculation recoverability:** Are equations, units, cost year, component costs, replacement/finance rules, and model artifacts available? Which parameters remain inferred or unresolved?
3. **Distinctiveness and importance:** Does the case add a decision perspective, physical architecture, accounting rule, or influential literature position not already represented?
4. **Benchmark compatibility:** Can it answer the predeclared common question without changing the historical model's logic? If only partially, state the supported subset.

Provisional priorities are the Charania–DePasquale 2007 company case, Kornuta 2019 company case, Jones 2019 cislunar campaign model, Sowers's 2020/2021 thermal-mining business-case family, and Pelech 2019 if full text is acquired. Treat Jones's 2019 lunar-surface and 2020 Moon/Mars papers as distinct cases within a versioned family. Bennett is best handled as a descendant reanalysis/architecture variant, while Blair 2002 is a serious historical candidate pending workbook discovery. This yields a shortlist of five structurally different baselines with Blair as a possible sixth; it is not a final inclusion decision. Do not count linked descendants as independent model “votes.” Include Metzger 2023 as a contemporary critique/analytical comparator even if excluded from the matched benchmark set.

## Source handling and next research actions

1. Save a stable copy of each cited primary paper/report and record retrieval date, edition, URL, DOI/NTRS/IAF/USRA identifier, checksum, and associated supplements in a model-specific evidence index. Keep originals immutable.
2. Search author/repository/NASA/USRA/IAF supplemental archives for spreadsheets, source code, presentations, appendices, cost-model output, and errata. An inaccessible proprietary package should be recorded as a limitation, not assumed reproducible.
3. Create one version record per decision case before coding: title/authors/date/version, customer, destination, product and state, system boundary, native metric, baseline case, core equations, and source page/figure/table.
4. Reconcile tables with page images where OCR may alter labels or values. In particular, inspect Sowers's source table imagery before resolving the flagged inconsistencies.
5. Build the parameter crosswalk before any common-input run. Define launch cost by destination and burden, cost year/currency, system lifetime, capacity factor, replacement logic, transportation losses, demand profile, and common product state.
6. Keep modern parameter ranges in a separate benchmark file and freeze its version before examining model outcomes.
